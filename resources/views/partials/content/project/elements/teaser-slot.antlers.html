{{# content/project/elements/teaser-slot — places one project teaser on the portfolio grid. The listing repeats a pattern of eight slots taken from the XD design (artboard 1920 wide, 12 columns of 90px with a 50px gutter and 145px margins, i.e. 1630px of content). Every width below is therefore expressed in columns rather than pixels, so the composition survives the site container being narrower (max-w-site is 1630px *including* its padding). Desktop slots (xl and up, `#n` = the project's position in the design): #1 cols 2–5 #2 cols 7–12, 208px lower #3 cols 1–8, bleeds left #4 cols 7–12, bleeds right #5 cols 3–11 #6 cols 7–12, bleeds right #7 cols 1–4, rides up beside #6 #8 cols 6–9 #1+#2 and #7+#8 share a grid row, and in both pairs the second teaser would be pushed onto a row of its own by auto-placement — each slot therefore carries an explicit row (`--row-xl`). Six rows per cycle of eight projects. The design interlocks #7 and #8 with the row above; #7 is paired with #6 there and #8 rides up into #7. Reproduced literally that puts #8's offset at the mercy of how tall #6's caption happens to be, and a long lead line makes the two collide. Pairing #7 with #8 instead gives the same picture — #7 still climbs up alongside #6 — but both offsets are now measured from #6's bottom, and neither slot can ever overlap something in its own columns. The vertical offsets are fractions of the grid width rather than Tailwind margins: a percentage margin on a grid item resolves against its own grid area, so it would shrink and grow with the slot's column span. `--mt-xl` and friends are multiplied by `--gw` in components/portfolio-grid.css, which is where the row and the margins are actually applied. The fractions are the design's pixel gaps over its 1630px of content (208 → .128, −552 → −.339, and a standard row gap of 150 → .092). Below xl the pairs are dropped and auto-placement takes over: a two-up staggered rhythm from md, a single gently offset column below that. The aspect ratios relax towards 3:2 / square / 3:4 so the crops stay sane on a phone.
